## Highlights

- Subtitles embedded in a video are read from the Matroska index, in the background: 0.6 s instead of a 21-second freeze on a 23 GB film.
- Kainote's items lead every menu, in Kainote's order; the rest is grouped into named submenus.
- Without video, the edit box is short and the grid gets the rest of the window, as in Kainote.

## Improved

### Menus and window like Kainote's

- **Menus.** Kainote's items are at the top of each menu, in its order. What Aegisub and FS Editor add sits in a few named submenus instead of one long list: "Import and export" in File, "Lines" in Edit, "Navigation", "Keyframes and timecodes", "Frame", "Zoom" and "Video window" in Video, "Tags", "Timing" and "Assistants" in Subtitles. The Video menu has 25 items instead of 55, Subtitles 14 instead of 30.
- **Panel keys.** The key lists of the edit box, video, timing and assistants (128 items) are no longer in the menus. Those commands still work and are in the "Hotkeys..." window, grouped by panel; a command with no default key has to be given one there.
- **Edit box without video.** It is short and the grid gets the rest of the window, as in Kainote. Before, it kept the video's height and took half the window. The heights with and without video are remembered separately.
- **Status bar.** Fields with nothing to show are hidden; without video there used to be seven empty boxes.
- **Shortcuts.** Ctrl+Q toggles "Show two tabs" (the counterpart of Kainote's subtitle preview). The other Kainote default shortcuts were already the same.

## Fixed

### Subtitles from a video no longer freeze the window

After choosing a video with subtitles inside, the window could stop responding: the subtitles were read on the window's thread, packet by packet, which meant reading through the whole video and audio along with them. On a 23 GB film with 124 kB of subtitles that was 21 seconds of "Not responding".

- A Matroska file has its own index (Cues) that points to every block of the subtitle track. Subtitles are now read from it: a seek and a few bytes per line.
- The index is trusted only where it is known to be complete (files from FFmpeg, or from mkvmerge when the number of entries matches the track's frame count), and every block has to be where the index says. Files without such an index, with compressed blocks, and WebVTT are read as before.
- Either way, reading runs in the background. If it takes longer than 300 ms, a progress window with a "Cancel" button appears, as in Aegisub.

| 23 GB film, SubRip subtitles | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| reading the subtitles, first time | 20.8 s | 0.6 s |
| reading the subtitles, next time | 20.8 s | 29 ms |
| longest window stall while opening | 20.8 s | under 0.1 s |

The subtitle text is the same as when read packet by packet (compared on this film and eight muxing variants).

## Known limitations

- macOS is not built or tested yet.

## Install

Unzip the package anywhere and run `bin/fsed` (`bin\fsed.exe` on Windows); there is nothing else to install. The packages were built by CI from the tagged commit; verify a download against `SHA256SUMS.txt`.
