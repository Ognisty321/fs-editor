AviSynth and VapourSynth scripts open as video and audio, the graphics card converts frame colours, and this release adds WebVTT, an update check and three edit-box and style conveniences from Kainote.

## Highlights

- `.avs` and `.vpy` scripts open like any other video and audio, using the AviSynth or VapourSynth installed on the system.
- YUV-to-RGB conversion runs on the graphics card: 4K HEVC 10-bit playback goes from 70 to 160 frames per second.
- WebVTT files can be read and saved.
- Help > "Check for updates..." looks for a newer release; the check at startup is off by default.

## New

### AviSynth and VapourSynth scripts

`.avs` and `.vpy` files open like any other video and audio: from the open dialog, by dragging them onto the window, and in `fsed media`.

- Neither library ships with the program. Each is loaded from what is installed on the system when the first script of its kind is opened; if it is missing, a message says what to install.
- VapourSynth is looked for in this order: the setting, `VSSCRIPT_PATH`, the registry, the default library names, and finally through Python. AviSynth is `avisynth.dll` from the program's folder or the system.
- Frames stay in the clip's format and take the same path as any other video, with the matrix, range and pixel aspect ratio from the frame properties.
- A VapourSynth script can supply timecodes and keyframes in `__aegi_timecodes` and `__aegi_keyframes`, as in Aegisub.
- Audio from AviSynth works as in Aegisub (down-mixer, 16-bit, sample rate); from VapourSynth in 16, 24 and 32 bits and float.
- New settings: "VapourSynth library (VSScript)", "Avisynth memory limit", "Allow Avisynth older than 2.6", "Avisynth down-mixer" and "Avisynth audio sample rate".

The AviSynth path is tested on AviSynth+ 3.7.6 (fourteen pixel formats, audio, errors), the VapourSynth path on six formats.

### WebVTT

`.vtt` files are read and saved: in the window, in `fsed`, and as a style source when importing. `<i>`, `<b>`, `<u>` tags and entities are kept, and cue placement (`line`, `align`) becomes `\an`. `NOTE`, `STYLE` and `REGION` blocks and cue identifiers are skipped.

### Update check

- Help > "Check for updates..." asks GitHub for the latest release. If it is newer, it shows its notes and three choices: open the download page, skip this version, remind me later.
- At startup the program checks only if that is turned on in the settings, once a day. It is off by default, and the program does not connect to the network on its own.
- The "About" window shows the version number.

### From Kainote

- **Matching words.** Double-clicking a word in the edit box highlights its other occurrences in the line: whole words, ignoring case, also right after `\N` and `\h`. The colour is part of the theme.
- **Tag descriptions.** The list that opens after typing `\` says what each tag does.
- **Style editor in the manager.** With "Edit styles in this window" checked, the editor sits next to the lists in the style manager window and stays open while moving from style to style. By default it opens in a separate window, as in Aegisub.

## Improved

### Frame colours on the graphics card

A playing frame used to be converted from YUV to RGB on the CPU, which took most of the time of a 4K frame. Now the program sends the decoder's planes to the graphics card as they are, and two shaders do the conversion, in the same order and with the same coefficients as before. libass subtitles are drawn separately, and only in the rectangle they cover.

| 4K HEVC 10-bit, one frame | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| decoding to the form the window shows | 14.3 ms | 6.3 ms |
| frames per second | 70 | 160 |

- The picture is the same: compared with the old path on BT.601 and BT.709 clips, limited and full range, 8 and 10 bits, it differs by at most one level, and window captures with subtitles by at most two.
- Frames the graphics card cannot take this way still go the old path: RGB, with an alpha channel, interlaced, and HDR10 with mastering display metadata, as does everything that needs RGB on the CPU (saving and copying a frame, the visual tools, `fsed media frame`).

### Audio panel while indexing

The audio panel appears as soon as a file is chosen, with the index percentage in the middle, a progress bar and the file name; before, it stayed hidden until indexing finished. "Close audio" also works while indexing and stops it.

## Under the hood

- CI unpacks and runs every package: the window with a file, video with audio, sixteen dialogs and the update offer. On Windows this test is required; on Linux it passed but for now only reports its result.
- The three largest source files (about 210 kB each) are split into modules, one per window, menu or area; behaviour, texts and shortcuts are unchanged.

## Known limitations

- macOS is not built or tested yet.

## Install

Unzip the package anywhere and run `bin/fsed` (`bin\fsed.exe` on Windows); there is nothing else to install. AviSynth+ or VapourSynth are needed only to open their scripts. The packages were built by CI from the tagged commit; verify a download against `SHA256SUMS.txt`.
